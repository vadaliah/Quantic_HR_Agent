POL-019 Working Hours and Flexible Arrangements

This policy covers when work is done and how flexible arrangements are agreed. Where work is done from is in POL-003; working abroad is in POL-004.

1. Standard Hours

The standard working week is 40 hours. Core hours are 10:00 to 15:00 in the employee's local time, during which employees should be reachable. The remainder of the day is arranged with the manager and the team.

An employee whose role is designated shift-based follows the published roster rather than the standard week, and the roster must be published at least 14 days in advance.

2. Breaks and Rest

Employees are entitled to a break of at least 20 minutes where the working day exceeds six hours. Breaks are not counted as working time for overtime-eligible roles. There must be at least 11 hours between the end of one working day and the start of the next.

3. Flexible Start and Finish

Employees may vary their start and finish times around the core hours without a formal agreement, provided the arrangement does not affect the team's coverage. A manager may require a fixed schedule where coverage requires it, and must give 30 days' notice of a change to an existing arrangement.

4. Compressed Hours

An employee may request compressed hours, such as a four-day week with longer days, after 12 months of service. Requests are made to the manager and reviewed by People Operations. Approval must not reduce total weekly hours below 40 for full-time roles, and the arrangement is reviewed after six months.

5. Part-time Work

Employees scheduled for fewer than 30 hours per week are part-time for the purposes of accrual and benefits; the accrual consequence is set out in POL-001 and the benefits consequence in POL-007. A request to move from full-time to part-time is made in writing and is not unreasonably refused.

6. Time Recording

All employees record time in the time tool. Non-exempt employees record start, finish, and break times; exempt employees record attendance and any leave taken. Records are kept for the retention period in POL-017.

Records must be completed by the end of the following week. A manager who cannot confirm a record must raise it with the employee rather than correcting it unilaterally.

7. Night and Weekend Work

Work outside the employee's normal pattern is permitted where the team has agreed it. Employees who work on a weekend are not entitled to a premium rate unless a site-specific agreement provides one; a substitute rest day may be given at the manager's discretion.

An employee required to work on a public holiday follows POL-002, including the pre-approval requirement.

8. Temporary Schedule Changes

A manager may change a shift rota with 14 days' notice. A change to a flexible arrangement under section 3 requires 30 days' notice. Where notice is shorter, the change is agreed with the employee in writing or is not made.

9. Ad Hoc Flexibility

An employee may adjust a single day, such as starting late after an appointment, without a formal arrangement, provided core hours are met or the time is made up within the same week and the team is told in advance.

Ad hoc flexibility must not be used to reduce total hours. Repeated use that affects coverage is handled as a performance matter under POL-014.

10. Recording Leave Within the Schedule

Leave taken under POL-001, POL-002, or POL-008 is recorded in the same tool and is visible to the manager. Time away must not be recorded as ordinary working time, and an employee must not work while recorded as absent.

11. Overtime Recording

All hours beyond the standard week must be recorded in the time tool by the end of the following week. Approval for overtime is given in advance where the need is foreseeable; emergency overtime is approved retrospectively within 14 days. Payment follows POL-018.

An employee must not work unpaid overtime. Where a manager asks for additional hours, the request and the reason are recorded in the time tool, whether or not the role is overtime-eligible.

12. Rest and Wellbeing

Managers must monitor patterns of long hours, and must act where an employee works more than 48 hours in a week or more than six consecutive days. Action means reducing the load or agreeing a rest period, not recording the exception and continuing.

13. Questions

Questions about hours are answered by the manager in the first instance and by People Operations where the arrangement is not agreed.
