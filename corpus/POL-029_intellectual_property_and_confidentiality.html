POL-029 Intellectual Property and Confidentiality

This policy covers who owns work product, what information is confidential, and what happens at the end of employment. Data classification levels are in POL-006 and its annex; this policy covers ownership and its consequences.

1. Ownership of Work Product

Work created by an employee in the course of their role belongs to the company. This includes code, documents, designs, and written material, whether or not it was created during working hours.

Work created outside the role, using the employee's own equipment and time, belongs to the employee, provided it does not use company confidential information and does not compete with the company. An employee who is unsure should disclose it before publishing.

2. Inventions and Ideas

An invention made by an employee in the course of their role, or using company information or equipment, belongs to the company. The employee must disclose it in writing to Legal within 30 days of making it.

Some jurisdictions limit what an employer may claim. Where local law is more protective of the employee, the local rule applies and Legal records the exception.

3. Confidential Information

Confidential information is any information that is not public and that the company treats as Confidential or Restricted under POL-006. It is not limited to documents; a conversation, a system design, or an unpublished plan can be confidential.

Confidential information may be used only for company work, only by those who need it, and only while the person is entitled to access it. The obligation survives the end of employment and has no fixed end date.

4. External Communications

Approval from the Communications team is required before any public statement, conference talk, article, or social media post that describes company work, a customer, or a product that has not been announced.

Speaking at an event about general industry topics does not require approval. Where an employee is identified as an Acme Corp employee, the material must be reviewed first.

5. Open Source and Third-Party Material

Company code must not incorporate a third-party component with a licence that conflicts with the company's licence, and must not publish company code under an open source licence without Legal approval.

Employees who contribute to an open source project outside work may do so on their own time, without company equipment, and must not use a company email address or identify the contribution as company work.

6. Leaving the Company

On the last working day, the employee must:

  1. Return all company equipment under POL-010.
  2. Delete company information from any personal device or account.
  3. Transfer work in progress to their manager, in the company systems.
  4. Stop using any company credential, and report any they cannot remove.

The obligation of confidentiality continues. An employee may describe their role and their general skills, but must not disclose confidential information or take customer lists, pricing, or code.

7. Breach

A breach of this policy is treated as a conduct matter under POL-026 and may be gross misconduct. Where a breach also involves personal data, POL-017 applies and the privacy lead must be told within 24 hours under POL-006.
