# POL-014 Performance and Development

This policy describes how performance is measured, how ratings are applied, and
how development and promotion decisions are made.

## 1. Review Cycle

Acme Corp runs two formal review cycles each year, in June and December.
Performance ratings cover the six months since the previous cycle. Employees who
have been in role for fewer than 90 days at the end of a cycle are not rated in
that cycle; their first rating falls in the following cycle.

## 2. Goals and Competencies

Every employee must have at least three and no more than six documented goals at
the start of each cycle. Goals are recorded in the performance tool by the end
of the second week of the cycle. A goal without a measurable outcome is not a
valid goal and must be rewritten.

Competencies are assessed separately from goals. The competency framework has
four levels and applies to all roles; the level expected for a role is recorded
on the role profile, not chosen by the manager.

## 3. Ratings

Ratings are assigned on a four-point scale: Does Not Meet, Partially Meets,
Meets, Exceeds. A rating of Exceeds requires written evidence against at least
two goals and one competency. Ratings above Meets are reviewed by the manager's
manager before they are released.

The distribution of ratings is not forced. Managers must not apply a quota, and
a rating must not be adjusted to fit a distribution.

## 4. Feedback and Improvement

Feedback is expected throughout the cycle, not only at review time. Where a
rating is below Meets, the manager must hold a documented feedback conversation
within 14 days of the rating being released, covering the specific shortfall and
the support available.

An employee may respond in writing to a rating within 14 days. The response is
retained with the rating and is visible to the reviewer for the next cycle.

## 5. Performance Improvement Plans

A performance improvement plan (PIP) runs for a minimum of 30 days and a maximum
of 90 days. A PIP must state the specific outcomes required, the support
provided, and the review dates. The employee must be told that failure to meet
the outcomes may lead to termination.

An employee on a PIP may request a review by People Operations if they believe
the plan is unclear or the support was not provided as stated.

## 6. Promotion

Promotion requires sustained performance at the next level, evidenced across at
least two consecutive cycles, and a business need for the role at that level. A
promotion is not granted solely because an employee has been in role for a long
time, and time in role is not by itself sufficient evidence.

Promotion decisions at manager level and above are reviewed by a calibration
panel. Calibration panels must include at least one member from outside the
reporting line.

## 7. Compensation Changes

Compensation changes take effect in the payroll cycle following approval, and
are communicated in writing. Out-of-cycle increases require director approval
and are limited to cases of role change, retention risk, or correction of an
error. Compensation questions are answered by People Operations; managers must
not commit to a figure before it is approved.
